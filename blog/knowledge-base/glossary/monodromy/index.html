Monodromy

The derivative of one full trip around a periodic orbit: the ordered product of the step Jacobians.

The monodromy matrix of a periodic orbit is the derivative of one full trip around the cycle. If the orbit is \(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_p\), with \(x_{k+1}=F(x_k)\) and \(x_{p+1}=x_1\), a tiny displacement at \(x_1\) is carried along by the Jacobian \(DF\) at each point. After one period the displacement has been multiplied by

\[ M = DF(x_p)\,DF(x_{p-1})\cdots DF(x_1). \]

The last step’s Jacobian stands on the left, because it acts last. The eigenvalues of \(M\) are the orbit’s multipliers. In a direction whose multiplier \(\mu\) satisfies \(\lvert\mu\rvert<1\), a small nudge shrinks over the cycle and the orbit attracts along that direction. A multiplier with \(\lvert\mu\rvert>1\) means the nudge grows, and the orbit is unstable along that direction. \(M\) is the discrete-time counterpart of a Floquet matrix.

One step can stretch while the cycle contracts. The monodromy is the product, so a large factor in a single Jacobian need not be a large multiplier.

Worked example

Let the two Jacobians be diagonal,

\[ DF(x_1)=\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad DF(x_2)=\begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix}. \]

The monodromy is

\[ M=DF(x_2)\,DF(x_1)=\begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix}. \]

A displacement \((u,v)\) at \(x_1\) returns as \((0.8u, 0.2v)\). The multipliers are \(0.8\) and \(0.2\). The factor \(2\) in the first Jacobian is real, and it is not a multiplier of the cycle.

def monodromy(jacobians):
    """Product DF(x_p) ... DF(x_1). jacobians are listed in visit order."""
    if len(jacobians) == 0:
        raise ValueError("a cycle needs at least one Jacobian")
    width = len(jacobians[0])
    product = [[1.0 if i == j else 0.0 for j in range(width)] for i in range(width)]
    for jacobian in jacobians:
        product = _matmul(jacobian, product)
    return product

def _matmul(left, right):
    width = len(left)
    return [
        [sum(left[i][k] * right[k][j] for k in range(width)) for j in range(width)]
        for i in range(width)
    ]

In this note the word is used for that cycle derivative, not for the monodromy of analytic continuation in complex analysis. A finite-precision product of many Jacobians can be inaccurate even when each factor looks accurate: the 8-step monodromy of one period-8 orbit is off by 3% in float32, while a single float32 Jacobian-vector product agrees to about \(5\times10^{-5}\).
